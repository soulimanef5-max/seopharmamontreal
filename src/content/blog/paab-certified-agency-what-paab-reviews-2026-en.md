"PAAB-certified agency" shows up often in the searches that bring people to this site. I understand why: if every piece of branded pharma advertising in Canada goes through PAAB, it seems natural that the agencies producing it would carry some PAAB credential. They don't. PAAB reviews advertising. It does not certify the agencies that make it.

That matters when you pick a partner, because it changes which claims you should trust and which questions you should ask.

The [Pharmaceutical Advertising Advisory Board](https://www.paab.ca/about) is an independent, not-for-profit review body. Health Canada recognizes its preclearance service for advertising aimed at healthcare professionals, and approved pieces carry the PAAB logo. On its own website, PAAB lists agencies as clients that submit advertising on behalf of the companies they work for. I found no certification or accreditation program for agencies anywhere on paab.ca.

---

## What PAAB is

PAAB describes itself as "an independent not-for-profit organization funded on a fee-for-service model" and "the only regulator whose preclearance service is recognized by Health Canada for advertising directed to healthcare professionals" ([PAAB, About](https://www.paab.ca/about)). Its standards are in the [PAAB Code of Advertising Acceptance](https://code.paab.ca/). Health Canada can send an official to PAAB board meetings as an ex-officio observer, but it keeps its own authority under the Food and Drugs Act.

If you want the practical side (whether review is mandatory, how a file moves through revision rounds), I wrote a separate walkthrough of the [PAAB review process](/en/blog/paab-review-process-explained-2026-en/).

## What PAAB reviews, and what it doesn't

PAAB's published scope covers promotional communication about prescription drugs, non-prescription drugs, biologics and natural health products when it is aimed at healthcare professionals, in any medium. For prescription drugs, PAAB also gives advisory opinions on material aimed directly at consumers.

Two things follow for anyone running a marketing plan. First, PAAB reviews a piece of advertising, not a company and not an agency. The approval number belongs to that piece. Second, unbranded educational content generally sits outside the promotional scope. Disease-awareness pages, regulatory explainers and category guides that don't promote a specific product are where most organic search and AI visibility is won. Where the line between advertising and information falls is covered in my guide to [prescription drug advertising rules in Canada](/en/blog/pharmaceutical-advertising-rules-canada-2026-en/).

## Why "PAAB-certified agency" is the wrong question

Since PAAB approves advertising and not firms, an agency that calls itself "PAAB-certified" is using loose wording. Sometimes that's harmless shorthand for "we submit to PAAB a lot." Sometimes it's meant to suggest a status that doesn't exist. Either way, it tells you nothing you can check.

PAAB's client list includes dozens of agencies, from global networks to Quebec firms. What they have in common is that they submit files. PAAB also runs workshops, which it says are open to anyone, plus paid consults. Going to a workshop is useful. It isn't accreditation.

A better question to ask a candidate: have you taken real files through PAAB, and can you keep most of my content out of the review queue in the first place?

## What a PAAB review costs in 2026

PAAB publishes its [fee schedule](https://www.paab.ca/fee-schedule) and a [fee calculator](https://www.paab.ca/fee-calculator.htm). In September 2026, the base fees for a preclearance review of material aimed at healthcare professionals or patients were:

| Review level | One language | English and French |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | $465 | $537 |
| ARO-10 | $572 | $644 |
| ARO-7 | $698 | $805 |
| ARO-4 | $930 | $1,073 |
| ARO-2 | $1,396 | $1,611 |

The same page sets out a few rules worth knowing before you budget. Fees go up every year with the cost of living, and HST is extra. PAAB invoices after the first review letter, and you pay for the review, whether or not the piece is accepted. Submissions over 10 pages or 15 references cost more, and so do files that need three or more resubmissions. Once a piece has its approval number, any later change is a new file with a new fee.

On timing, PAAB's [Services page](https://www.paab.ca/about-services.htm) lists a first review in 10 days for standard material and 3 days for each revision. The fastest paid option, ARO-2, targets a first response within 2 business days for smaller submissions.

In my experience the fee is rarely what hurts. The time your branded material spends in review is. That's why the way you structure your content matters more than which speed option you buy.

## How to choose a partner in Canada

Whether you're looking at a full-service agency, a medical writer or an SEO consultant, I'd check five things.

1. Ask about files, not badges. How many PAAB submissions have they handled, for which kinds of products, and how many revision rounds did a typical file take?
2. Ask them where the line sits between your branded and unbranded content. A good partner can tell you which of your pages need review and which don't, before anyone writes a word.
3. Make sure their frame of reference is Canadian. Advice built on FDA or HIPAA habits doesn't carry over, and my comparison of [PAAB and FDA](/en/blog/paab-vs-fda-seo-pharma-canada-2026-en/) shows where US playbooks break.
4. For natural health products, check that they know claims have to stay within what the product licence allows. I cover that in [what an NPN lets you say](/en/blog/nhp-claims-what-you-can-say-canada-2026-en/).
5. Be careful with guarantees. Nobody can guarantee a PAAB approval, a Google ranking or a citation in ChatGPT. If someone promises you all three, that tells you something about how they work.

## Where SEO and AI visibility fit

PAAB review and search visibility are different jobs, but they touch the same pages. What works for Canadian pharma brands is to separate two layers. The first is an unbranded educational hub: condition guides, category explainers, regulatory content. It carries most of the search demand, and it's the kind of page ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews tend to cite. The second is a lean branded layer, meaning product pages and professional campaigns, submitted to PAAB early and kept tight.

Set up that way, you keep publishing at a normal pace without sliding into regulated promotion. Helping pharma, natural health product and medical device companies build that structure is most of what I do as a [PAAB-aware SEO consultant](/en/seo-pharma/).
