If you searched for a "PAAB-certified agency", you are not alone: it is one of the most common ways Canadian pharma marketers start looking for help. The short answer surprises many people. **PAAB reviews advertising. It does not certify agencies.**

That distinction matters when you choose a partner, because it tells you which claims to trust and which questions to ask.

> **In short:** The [Pharmaceutical Advertising Advisory Board (PAAB)](https://www.paab.ca/about) is an independent, not-for-profit review body. Health Canada recognizes its preclearance service for advertising directed to healthcare professionals, and approved materials carry the PAAB logo. PAAB's own site lists agencies as **clients that submit advertising on behalf of sponsoring companies**. It describes no certification or accreditation program for agencies. What you should look for instead is proven submission experience and a clear content strategy.

---

## What PAAB is, in one paragraph

PAAB describes itself as "an independent not-for-profit organization funded on a fee-for-service model" and "the only regulator whose preclearance service is recognized by Health Canada for advertising directed to healthcare professionals" ([PAAB, About](https://www.paab.ca/about)). Its standards are set out in the [PAAB Code of Advertising Acceptance](https://code.paab.ca/). Health Canada is invited to attend PAAB board meetings as an ex-officio observer, without giving up any of its own authority under the Food and Drugs Act.

For the practical side of submitting (whether review is mandatory and how a file moves through revision rounds), see our walkthrough of the [PAAB review process](/en/blog/paab-review-process-explained-2026-en/).

## What PAAB reviews, and what it doesn't

PAAB's published scope is precise:

- **Reviewed:** promotional health product communication for **prescription, non-prescription, biological and natural health products** directed to **healthcare professionals**, in all media.
- **Advisory only:** PAAB also provides advisory comments on **direct-to-consumer materials for prescription drugs**.

Two consequences for marketers:

1. **The unit of review is the piece of advertising**, not the company or the agency that produced it. An approval number belongs to a specific advertising piece.
2. **Unbranded educational content generally sits outside that promotional scope.** Disease-awareness pages, regulatory explainers and category guides that do not promote a specific product are where most of your organic search and AI visibility is won. Where the line falls between advertising and information is covered in our guide to [prescription drug advertising rules in Canada](/en/blog/pharmaceutical-advertising-rules-canada-2026-en/).

## Why "PAAB-certified agency" is the wrong question

Because PAAB certifies advertising and not people or firms, a "PAAB-certified" label on an agency is at best loose wording and at worst a misleading claim. PAAB's client list includes dozens of agencies, from global networks to Quebec firms. What they share is that they submit files, not that they hold a status.

The better question is: **"Has this partner taken real files through PAAB, and do they know how to keep most of my content out of the review queue?"**

PAAB also runs training workshops and consults for clients. Attending them is useful, but it is training, not accreditation.

## What a PAAB review costs in 2026

PAAB publishes its [fee schedule](https://www.paab.ca/fee-schedule) and a [fee calculator](https://www.paab.ca/fee-calculator.htm). At the time of writing (September 2026), the base fees for a preclearance review directed to healthcare professionals or patients were:

| Review level | One language | English and French |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | $465 | $537 |
| ARO-10 | $572 | $644 |
| ARO-7 | $698 | $805 |
| ARO-4 | $930 | $1,073 |
| ARO-2 | $1,396 | $1,611 |

A few rules from the same page are worth knowing:

- Fees are **indexed to the cost of living every year** and **exclude HST**.
- Fees are invoiced after the first review letter and pay for **the review, not for acceptance**.
- Long submissions (more than 10 pages or 15 references) and files needing three or more resubmissions carry extra fees.
- Once a piece has its approval number, any later revision is a **new file with a new fee**.

The fastest option, **ARO-2**, targets a first response within 2 business days for smaller submissions. In practice, the bigger cost is rarely the fee. It is the calendar time that branded material spends in review, which is why content architecture matters more than speed options.

## How to choose a partner in Canada

Whether you need a full-service agency, a medical writer or an SEO consultant, these five checks separate real experience from a label:

1. **Ask about files, not badges.** How many PAAB submissions have they handled, for which product types, and what was the typical number of revision rounds?
2. **Ask where they draw the line between branded and unbranded content.** A good partner can explain which of your pages need review and which do not, before anything is written.
3. **Check for a Canadian frame of reference.** Advice built on FDA or HIPAA habits does not transfer. Our comparison of [PAAB and FDA](/en/blog/paab-vs-fda-seo-pharma-canada-2026-en/) shows where US playbooks break.
4. **For natural health products, check claims fluency.** Claims must stay within what the product licence authorizes. See [what an NPN lets you say](/en/blog/nhp-claims-what-you-can-say-canada-2026-en/).
5. **Be wary of guarantees.** Nobody can guarantee a PAAB approval, a Google ranking or an AI citation. Someone who promises all three is telling you something about their standards.

## Where SEO and AI visibility fit

PAAB review and search visibility are two different jobs that touch the same pages. The pattern that works for Canadian pharma brands:

- **Unbranded educational hub:** condition guides, category explainers and regulatory content. This layer carries most of the search demand and is what ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews tend to cite.
- **Lean branded layer:** product pages and professional campaigns, submitted early and kept tight.
